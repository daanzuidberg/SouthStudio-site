02The premise

A small paradox, and the reasoning that resolves it.

You are a new field investigator at the Time Correction Agency. Every case opens with a short scene: a date that doesn’t add up, a witness who couldn’t have been there, an object that shouldn’t exist yet.

Your job is to find the single alteration in the record and reason your way to the one timeline that holds. Nothing depends on what you know about history. Everything you need is in the evidence.

03How it works

Read the file. Work the evidence. Restore the timeline.

01

Read the file

Each case is a dossier: a scene, a directive, and a set of evidence records. Some records are true, some are the alteration.

02

Work the evidence

The tool is a logic grid. Mark ✓ where two facts hold together; one mark rules out the rest of that row and column. No dates to memorise.

03

Restore the timeline

When every fact points to one box, a single chronology remains. Check it against the fully worked solution at the back.

04Five clearances

From a coffee-break warm-up to a genuine head-scratcher.

The eighty cases are ordered in five clearances. Each clearance adds evidence, categories and misdirection; none of them adds trivia.

    07For whom

    For people who would rather reason than remember.

    Timeline is for anyone who likes the moment a problem clicks: crossword solvers, detective readers, people who finish the logic puzzle before the flight lands. You do not need to know history. You need to be willing to look carefully.

    What makes it interesting is the constraint. Every case has exactly one answer, and it can only be reached by deduction. There is no trivia to know and no luck to have, so when the timeline holds, it is because you made it hold.
